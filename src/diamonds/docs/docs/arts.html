ARTS — Sealed Beauty & Harmony ​

The Framework: Art domains where the mathematics is sealed. Not subjective taste — objective ratios, harmonies, and structures that humans find beautiful.

Each art in trinity structure:

  1. SEALED — proof of the ratio/harmony/structure (exact arithmetic)
  2. HONEST BOUNDARY — what the theorem cannot prove (why this ratio feels beautiful)
  3. AESTHETIC PAIR — the opening question (does math explain art or reduce it?)

The Domains ​

Colour (8 theorems) ​

Sealed: The colour wheel is ℤ/12 (12 hues, wrapping). Complementary hues sit opposite (+6 half-turn, self-inverse involution). Triads are thirds (+4 landing {0,4,8}). Squares are fourths (+3 landing {0,3,6,9}). True colour is 8 bits a channel (2^24 = 16.7M colours).

Honest Boundary: No proof that complementary colours are "beautiful" — only that they are symmetric.

Aesthetic Question: Does the colour wheel explain why red and green clash beautifully, or is math just a description?

Music & Production (10 theorems) ​

Sealed: The chromatic scale is ℤ/12 (octave wraps). An octave doubles frequency (440→880 Hz). The circle of fifths is one cycle (7 semitones coprime to 12 visits all 12). Reversing a clip is self-inverse. Phase inversion (x ↦ −x) is self-inverse. Their fusion reverse-then-invert is itself an involution.

Honest Boundary: No proof that a major chord is "happy" or minor "sad" — only that they have different ratios.

Aesthetic Question: If we understand the mathematics of harmony perfectly, does music lose its magic?

Typography (14 theorems) ​

Sealed: The printer's units close exactly (6 picas = 72 points = 1 inch). The em is the type's own square (12pt type has a 12pt em). The 3:4:5 rectangle is harmonious (its diagonal is whole: 3²+4²=5²). Fibonacci pages approach the golden ratio (5²−3·8 = 1). The readable measure is 66 characters (inside the 45–75 range typographers keep). A ream is 500 sheets (20 quires of 25).

Honest Boundary: No proof that a 3:4:5 page is "more beautiful" than a 1:√2 page — only that it satisfies Pythagorean closure.

Aesthetic Question: Does knowing that a beautiful page follows the 3-4-5 triangle make it more or less beautiful?

Photography (8 theorems) ​

Sealed: Shutter speeds are powers of 2 (1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/64). Apertures are f-stops (f/1, f/1.4, f/2, etc.). The standard rounds: 1/125 ≈ 1/128, f/1.4 ≈ √2. Reciprocity is exact addition of stops (1 stop of shutter = 1 stop of aperture). ISO doublings are exact powers of 2 (100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200).

Honest Boundary: No proof that a "correctly exposed" photo is beautiful — only that it follows the photographic standard.

Aesthetic Question: Is a perfectly exposed photograph better than an imperfectly exposed one that moves you?

The Spectrum (8 theorems) ​

Sealed: Wavelength × frequency = c (light speed, exact). The visible window is one octave (700nm/400nm ≈ 1.75 < 2). The SEVEN bands (radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma) ordered by frequency. Photon energy E = h·f.

Honest Boundary: No proof that visible light is "special" — only that it's where evolution happened.

Aesthetic Question: Why does human vision peak in the green part of the spectrum? Is it accident or design?

Creating Art from Math ​

Each arts folder contains:

  1. Proof — link to sealed theorem
  2. Interactive tool — explore the ratio/harmony (coming)
  3. Gallery — examples from nature and culture
  4. Paradox — the open question about math and beauty

The Honest Scope: These theorems describe STRUCTURE that humans find beautiful. They do NOT prove beauty or explain why your heart moves. Math is the language of art, not its soul.

Why Arts Matter ​

A sealed arts theorem proves:

  • Patterns exist — measurable, verifiable, recomputable
  • Humans gravitate to them — the colour wheel, the golden ratio, consonant intervals
  • Structure and beauty are linked — but not identical

But it cannot prove:

  • Why we love these patterns (evolution? culture? accident?)
  • Whether mathematical beauty is objective or projected
  • What beauty is

The paradox: The most mathematical art (architecture, music composition, photography) often feels the most alive. Yet explaining the math does not diminish the magic — it deepens it.

# The trading floor <Badge type="tip" text="work-units, never money" />

> This is the shelf that took longest to build, and the delay was the point. A "trading" floor in a ledger that
> mints coins could mean two very different things, and only one of them is honest: **this floor measures work,
> not worth.** A coin here is a *measured unit of work saved* — two per verified exchange, conserved by
> [`two_coins`](/theorem/two_coins) — and it is not money, not a security, not a holding, and not anyone's
> wealth. Nothing on this page is financial advice, a valuation, or a forecast.

## The four instruments

**The desk** prices a workload: how many coins the ledger's own billing charges for a set of events. It imports
that billing rather than reimplementing it, so the price a reader computes here is the price the tree charges
everywhere ([`one_source_is_exactly_one`](/theorem/one_source_is_exactly_one)).

**The census** counts what has been minted — two coins per sealed thing — and *checks* the conservation instead
of assuming it: the rate must be exactly two at every scale, or the census says so.

**The leverage** measures what an existing receipt spares a verifier: contribute the two, save up to the
sixty-four — and a measured saving **never goes below zero**, which is a sealed floor rather than a courtesy.

**The compound** reads the rate at which a growing ledger spares re-derivation: 55/54 per sealed exchange — the
tenth triangle over the sequence group's own order, a superparticular comma — first doubling at seal thirty-eight
([`advantage_first_doubles_at_seal_38`](/theorem/advantage_first_doubles_at_seal_38)), proven in exact integers
with no logarithm anywhere. The ratio stays a fraction on this page because the ledger does not round what it
can carry whole.

## Why the difference mints and the ratio bills

The pair the double torus handed this ledger — 110 and 108 — carries two roles at once, and separating them is
the whole of the billing law ([`difference_mints_ratio_bills`](/theorem/difference_mints_ratio_bills)): their
**difference** is the conserved coin of every exchange, and their **ratio**, reduced by the coins themselves, is
55/54 — unit-free, and therefore the same rate in any currency, which is precisely why it is not a currency.
Subtraction is the coin falling into the purse; the ratio is the purse growing.

## Honest scope

Work-units and ratios over verified exchanges. **No money, no market, no advice, no forecast, no claim about
anyone's finances** — and the bound is not a promise but a sealed refusal: what this floor will not settle stays open
rather than guessed ([`silence_never_refutes`](/theorem/silence_never_refutes)), exactly as every unproven claim
does here. Every instrument on this floor carries that scope inside its own answer — a test enforces
it — so the statement cannot be lost when a figure is quoted somewhere else. If you want to know what a proof
costs or what a receipt spares, this floor answers exactly. If you want to know what anything is *worth*, it
has nothing to say, deliberately.

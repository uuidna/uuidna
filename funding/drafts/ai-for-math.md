## Summary

uuidna is a corpus of 2575 distinct mathematical propositions, each proven in Lean 4 and each closed without relying on a single axiom. Every proof is content-addressed, every published claim links the proof that earns it, and the whole corpus recomputes from source: a reader who trusts nobody can re-derive it.

**Why this route.** Machine-checkable mathematics at corpus scale is the fund's stated subject.

**What the funded work would add.** More of the corpus brought under the same discipline — new domains formalised to the axiom-free standard, each with the generator that produces it, the falsifier that can refute it, and a published monograph carrying its proofs — plus the tooling that makes the corpus usable by others: the deposit and citation layer, the cross-repository merge key that lets two projects holding one result publish once rather than twice, and the audit chain that refuses a claim before it ships.

## Licensing of the funded deliverables

**The deliverables of this proposal would be released under CC-BY-4.0 (documents and scientific outputs) and Apache-2.0 (code), in their entirety.**

This is stated plainly because the project's EXISTING archive carries a different licence, and reviewers should not have to discover that. The archive is CC-BY-NC-ND-4.0, which carries NC (NonCommercial), failing the freedom to use for any purpose; and ND (NoDerivatives), failing the freedom to modify, and redistribute modified versions. Neither restriction is compatible with a recognised free/open licence, so the archive as it stands would not satisfy the requirement that funded outputs be open in their entirety.

The proposal therefore scopes the funded work as SEPARATE, openly-licensed deliverables that cite the existing corpus rather than re-license it. Concretely: new formalisation work, its proofs, its generators and its published papers would be CC-BY-4.0/Apache-2.0 from the first commit, in a form any reader may use, modify and redistribute commercially. Nothing in the funded scope depends on the restricted licence, and no deliverable would be dual- or mixed-licensed.

## What is claimed, and whose claim is whose

Both halves at full strength. The results below are CLAIMED outright — 2575 propositions, each closed by the Lean 4 kernel over its own finite domain with an empty trust base. What belongs to others is credited to them, and what no formal method can reach is named so a reviewer does not have to guess at the boundary:

- **The finite instances are claimed; the open conjectures are not solved by anyone here.** `decide` settles a decidable proposition over a FINITE domain, and every famous open conjecture quantifies over an infinite one — so no such proof exists in this corpus or can. What the corpus DOES hold, and claims, are finite instances and counting facts adjacent to those problems, each scoped in its own theorem name.
- **No quantum-computational speedup.** The project's advantage claims are about verification costing less than recomputation, in measured magnitudes, on ordinary hardware.
- **No claim that the formalised domains are complete.** A monograph proves what it proves and says so.
- **No peer-review status.** The corpus is archived and openly readable; that is provenance, not review.
